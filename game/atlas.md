# Thai fishing atlas

Release 0.3 contains 117 selectable locations with stable numeric IDs. The initial four preserve the legacy progression families. Mae Ngat Reservoir replaces the imagined Chiang Mai Pond. The 96 inland entries name real rivers, creeks, lakes, wetlands and reservoirs in the Thai Department of Fisheries guide, table 5. The 21 saltwater entries use named provincial fishing waters within the department's Gulf and Andaman reporting areas. They identify stretches of coast rather than individual fishing piers. Each record has an independently written fictional scene and its supporting geographic source.

Five illustrated habitat backgrounds have dawn, day, dusk and night palettes. Scores reuse regional themes with deterministic local arrangements. Encounter pools and catch weights are game designs. Blue-clawed river prawns appear in the lowland river pool at dusk/night. Freshwater stingrays appear in the big-river pool at dawn/night. Saltwater adds ten marine species including queenfish, cobia, giant trevally and sailfish. The journal records counts and best length across 52 species. Weight is derived from species and length. Story lines are fiction and the atlas is a game, not an access or angling-permit directory.
