## Numbered sheets

Make a 4 column by 4 row (sixteen-cell) production fish specimen plate in exactly the attached Thai Waters reference style. Transparent background. Every cell contains ONE complete, separately drawn, right-facing real species in strict row-major order. All fish centered with clear gutters and padding, no labels, lines or numbers. Realistic volumes, anatomical markings, jaws, gill covers, eye, individual scales, fin rays and subtle warm dorsal illumination in the reference palette, with a thin single cream edge. Accurate species and family silhouettes: refer to the Latin binomial and its family. Absolutely no generic oval fish, no invented color on naturally silver fish. Permit dorsal view of flattened rays and a vertical body for seahorses. All heads face right, tails at left. Keep full fin and snout inside own cell. Render at high resolution before native 96 x 50 reduction.

Append the scientific names with their families in four rows of four. See the [compiler](../compile-field.cjs) for the final color reduction and sizing.

## Twelve-species comparison sheet

The original [first game plate](../plate-0.png) was the source reference for the comparison batch, prior to selection of plate 2.

Create a production natural-history specimen sprite plate for the Thai Waters handheld fishing game. Use the supplied original plate ONLY as the style reference: detailed, lifelike side-profile fish illustration with carefully modeled highlighted scales, recognizable mouth, operculum, fin-ray anatomy, gentle warm highlight and desaturated natural shadow. New plate: landscape 4 columns by 3 equal rows on genuine transparent alpha, exactly 12 separate fish, centered one per cell, complete noses tails and fins, all heads on RIGHT. Large clear transparent gutters between rows and columns. No words or labels, no water, no boxes and no shadows behind them. Each is an individually painted scientific-style fish in game colors, for eventual reduction to 96 x 50 pixels. The 12 subjects in reading order:
ROW 1: Abalistes stellaris starry triggerfish (angular sloping forehead, granular olive reticulation and pale spots, three dark dorsal saddle patches); Acanthurus tristis adult Indian mimic surgeonfish (olive brown disk, orange around pectoral base, fine blue fin edging, white caudal spine); Chitala borneensis Bornean featherback (silvery high arched humped shoulder, knife-shaped tapering tail flowing into impossibly long rippled anal fin, tiny dorsal tuft); Stolephorus insularis Indian anchovy (slender translucent silver, fine bright silver lateral stripe, deeply forked clear tail, large eye).
ROW 2: Acanthurus bariene black-spot surgeonfish (deep oval grey-brown, small distinct black mark behind eye, striated blue fin rim); Acanthurus japonicus powder-brown tang (dark brown disk, white cheek below eye, orange-red patch on rear of dorsal, yellow trim and white tail base); Balistoides conspicillum clown triggerfish (black, huge white spots across belly, golden honeycomb saddle, yellow-orange mouth); Cephalopholis leopardus leopard hind (stout red-orange grouper, round tail, many tiny blue jewel spots).
ROW 3: Fusigobius pallidus pale goby (bottom-resting slim tan with tiny rusty dots and large head, separated dorsals); Hypsibarbus salweenensis Salween barb (silver-gold sides and deep individually edged scales, golden paired fins, forked tail); Schistura aurantiaca orange-finned loach (long low loach body, beige with narrow chocolate bars and soft orange tail, tiny mouth barbels); Zenarchopterus buffonis Buffon's river garfish (thin, long projecting lower jaw, silver body, posterior dorsal, tapered tail).
Keep every fish as detailed as the individual fish on the reference. Frame ultra-long shapes inside their own cell.
