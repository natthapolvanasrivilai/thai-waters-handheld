# Thai Waters phone playtest

Open [Thai Waters](https://thai-waters-pocket-angler.shoung0690.chatgpt.site) in your phone browser and sign in with the ChatGPT account that owns the private Site. The QR alongside this guide opens the same HTTPS address. The game runs over Wi-Fi or cellular.

1. The phone opens in screen-only view. Rotate to landscape to fill the available viewport. Tap the fullscreen icon in the LCD header to request browser fullscreen where supported. All game settings and the fish collection open within the LCD. Choose **Device view** to try the Rev F top-left power key, folding right-side crank and removable rod grip.
2. Open **Menu → Rod workshop** and choose a technique. Float drift asks you to distinguish a nibble from a bite. The spoon needs a short wind followed by a pause. The jig needs a lift followed by a drop. Each technique has on-screen cues.
3. Tap the water where you want the float to land. The center channel is clear. Pause and lift to free a snagged branch, or lower the rod and use **Pay out** at a rock. Reel in debris to identify it. For a fish, tap to strike, then drag the round knob clockwise to retrieve. When the fish runs, ease off or pay out. When it swims toward you, take up slack quickly. A landed fish moves into the collection.
4. For rod motion, open **Menu → Motion bench → Enable motion** in the phone browser and grant access. Hold the phone comfortably until **Centered** appears, then resume. Lift about 20° and return to the starting angle in one smooth sweep to cast. The first lift teaches the game your grip direction. Rotating between portrait and landscape starts centering again. **Recenter** and **Set custom lift** are available for a different grip or range. The bench shows the incoming sensor source and live rod angle. If a preview blocks sensor access, open the private HTTPS link directly in your browser.
   To wind the reel, trace the flat knob's rim or the 3D grip with one finger. In screen-only view you can also hold the knob's center for steady retrieval. For hands-free winding, turn on **Wrist reel** in Motion bench. Lift once to teach the rod direction. While working a spoon or fighting a catch, keep the rod low, twist the phone sideways about 25°, then return near center. Each outward stroke winds half a turn. Straight lifts continue to control the rod. The bench shows the live roll angle, accepted turns and sensor sample count.
5. To try a hosted shared event, open the private Site in two separate browser profiles or on two phones signed into the owner account. Choose the same fishing spot and join **Menu → Shared event** in each. The goal is eight catches per three-minute round.
6. Tap **Enable sound** over the water for a two-note check. Open **Menu → Music & bite cues → Test sound** to replay it or recover after switching apps. The iPhone uses media playback volume and the currently selected audio output.
7. In iPhone Safari, tapping **Sound with bite cues** changes the setting and, on iOS 18 or later with system haptics enabled, can produce a native switch tick. Safari exposes no timed vibration for fish fights. **Prawn pick**, **Bottom hold** and **Fast run** audition their rhythms through sound and light. On vibration-capable Android browsers, **Test phone vibration** requests two pulses and the rod rumble control sets the level. Explicit Off persists.
8. The tackle box, collection, rod workshop and settings open over the right side of the fishing location. The water and rod continue animating while your catch clock pauses.

The collection, rod setup and settings are saved in the browser on each device. A real handheld uses its own save. The hosted browser event and the physical prototype’s local-network event host run separate rooms. The battery badge on the phone is marked SIM and can be changed in Motion bench.
